{"title":"S3 Object Lock — WORM Compliance","tags":["object-lock","worm","compliance","governance-mode"],"html":"<div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.5rem\">S3 Object Lock implements <strong>Write Once, Read Many (WORM)</strong> storage: once locked, an object version cannot be deleted or overwritten for a fixed period, or indefinitely under a legal hold. It is the feature that turns S3 from \"very durable storage\" into storage that can satisfy regulatory retention mandates such as SEC Rule 17a-4(f), FINRA, or CFTC requirements, and it underpins genuine ransomware protection because it makes deletion <strong>categorically impossible</strong>, not just access-controlled.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">Prerequisite: Versioning</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Object Lock <strong>can only be enabled on a bucket that has Versioning enabled</strong>, and in practice it must be enabled either at bucket creation time or by first enabling Object Lock support before Versioning is turned on in some flows — the key operational fact is that Object Lock and Versioning are inseparable. This is because retention settings and legal holds attach to a specific <strong>object version</strong>, not to a key generically; without a version identifier there is nothing concrete for the lock to reference. A side effect: locked objects can still be \"deleted\" in the loose sense of adding a delete marker (the current version becomes hidden from default GETs), but the underlying locked version itself remains physically present and protected until its retention expires.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; This is designed for cases where an organization wants strong default protection but still needs a documented, audited emergency-override path for a small set of trusted administrators.</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">In <strong>Compliance mode</strong>, there is <strong>no override mechanism whatsoever</strong> — not through IAM, not through the root user, not through AWS Support. Once a version is locked in Compliance mode with a retain-until date, it is guaranteed immutable until that date passes, full stop. This is the mode regulators specifically require because it must be provable that not even AWS itself, let alone the account owner, can manipulate the retention.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">Retention Period vs Retain-Until Date vs Legal Hold</h4>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>Retention period.</strong> A duration (days or years) applied to an object version, computed into a <strong>retain-until date</strong> stored on the object. The object cannot be deleted or overwritten until that date passes.</li>\n<li><strong>Retain-until date.</strong> The concrete timestamp derived from the retention period; it can be extended (pushed further out) in either mode, but in Compliance mode it can <strong>never be shortened</strong>, matching the \"cannot be weakened\" guarantee regulators need.</li>\n<li><strong>Legal hold.</strong> An entirely independent protection mechanism with <strong>no expiration date</strong> — it blocks deletion/overwrite for as long as it is applied, regardless of whether a retention period exists or has expired. Legal holds can be <strong>applied or removed at any time</strong> by any principal with the <code>s3:PutObjectLegalHold</code> permission, independent of Governance/Compliance mode and independent of retention state — this decoupling is exactly why legal holds are used for open-ended litigation holds where the end date is unknown in advance, distinct from a fixed regulatory retention period.</li>\n</ul>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">Interview Scenario: 7-Year Immutable Financial Records</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">\"A company must retain financial records for 7 years, and nobody — including administrators — should be able to delete them early.
