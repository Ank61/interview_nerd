{"title":"S3 Glacier Deep Dive","tags":["S3","Glacier","Archival","Storage Classes"],"html":"<div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.5rem\"> S3's <strong>Glacier</strong> family of storage classes is purpose-built for long-term archival data that is accessed rarely or almost never, trading retrieval speed for dramatically lower storage cost. There are three archive tiers to know cold for an interview — <strong>S3 Glacier Instant Retrieval</strong>, <strong>S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval</strong>, and <strong>S3 Glacier Deep Archive</strong> — and each occupies a distinct point on the retrieval-time-versus-cost spectrum.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">S3 Glacier Instant Retrieval</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Designed for archive data that is accessed rarely — perhaps once a quarter — but when it is needed, it must be available <strong>immediately</strong>. Retrieval latency is <strong>milliseconds</strong>, identical to S3 Standard, because objects remain instantly readable without an explicit restore step. This convenience comes at a price: it is the <strong>most expensive of the three archive tiers</strong> for storage, though still substantially cheaper than S3 Standard or S3 Standard-IA. It carries a <strong>minimum storage duration of 90 days</strong> — deleting or transitioning an object before that incurs a pro-rated early-deletion charge. Good fits include medical images or user-generated content that's rarely viewed after upload but must never make a user wait when it is.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Formerly just called \"S3 Glacier,\" this tier is for archives where an explicit wait for retrieval is acceptable. Unlike Instant Retrieval, objects here must be <strong>restored</strong> before they can be read, and you choose a retrieval speed tier at request time:</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>Expedited.</strong> Typically 1–5 minutes, at the highest per-GB retrieval cost of the three speed options, for urgent occasional need.</li>\n<li><strong>Standard.</strong> Typically 3–5 hours, at moderate retrieval cost — the default, balanced choice for planned restores.</li>\n<li><strong>Bulk.</strong> Typically 5–12 hours, at the lowest retrieval cost, well suited to restoring large volumes of data where timing isn't urgent.</li>\n</ul>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Storage cost for Flexible Retrieval is lower than Instant Retrieval, since you're trading away instant access for cheaper storage and a more flexible/cheaper retrieval cost structure depending on how urgently you need the data back. It carries a <strong>minimum storage duration of 90 days</strong> as well.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">S3 Glacier Deep Archive</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">The <strong>cheapest storage class AWS offers</strong>, designed for data that will almost certainly never be read again but must be retained for compliance, legal, or historical reasons — think 7-to-10-year regulatory retention of financial or legal records. Standard retrieval time is within <strong>12 hours</strong>, with a Bulk retrieval option that can take longer at even lower cost. It carries the longest <strong>minimum storage duration of the three tiers, 180 days</strong> — early deletion before that incurs a larger pro-rated charge relative to its very low storage price. Deep Archive is the correct default answer whenever an interview scenario describes data that is stored \"just in case\" and essentially never expected to be touched.</p>\n<div style=\"overflow-x: auto; margin-top: 0.75rem\">\n<table style=\"width: 100%; border-collapse: collapse; font-size: 0.92rem\">\n<thead><tr style=\"text-align: left\" data-box=\"1\"><th style=\"padding: 0.6rem; border: 1px solid #e2e8f0\" data-box=\"1\">Tier</th><th style=\"padding: 0.6rem; border: 1px solid #e2e8f0\" data-box=\"1\">Retrieval Time</th><th style=\"padding: 0.6rem; border: 1px solid #e2e8f0\" data-box=\"1\">Relative Retrieval Cost</th><th style=\"padding: 0.6rem; border: 1px solid #e2e8f0\" data-box=\"1\">Min. Restoring creates a <strong>temporary, readable copy</strong> of the object for a duration you specify (e.g., 1 to several days) in the restore request; the underlying archived copy remains in Glacier. Once the specified number of days elapses, the temporary restored copy automatically expires and is removed, reverting the object to its archived, non-readable state — you'd need to issue another restore request to read it again. This matters operationally: a restore is not a permanent state change, and forgetting to set an adequate expiration window (or re-restoring after it lapses) is a common operational gotcha.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">Interview Scenario: Legal Records Retention</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\"><strong>\"A company needs to store legal records for 10 years and almost never reads them. What would you choose?\"</strong> The complete answer combines three pieces:</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>S3 Glacier Deep Archive</strong> for storage — because the data is almost never accessed and needs to be retained for a very long, multi-year period, Deep Archive's rock-bottom storage cost is the right economic fit; the 12-hour retrieval time is an acceptable trade-off for data that's essentially \"insurance,\" not operational.</li>\n<li><strong>S3 Object Lock</strong> in <strong>compliance mode</strong> (or governance mode, depending on internal policy flexibility needed) — legal records typically must be immutable and protected from deletion or modification for the retention period, satisfying WORM (write-once-read-many) requirements that regulators or legal holds often demand; Object Lock enforces this at the object level regardless of IAM permissions, even against the root user in compliance mode.</li>\n<li><strong>Lifecycle policies</strong> to automate the transition — rather than manually managing storage class changes, a lifecycle rule can automatically transition objects from S3 Standard (during an initial active period, if any) down through Standard-IA and eventually into Deep Archive after a defined number of days, and can also enforce expiration/deletion after the full 10-year retention period if legally permitted, minimizing both cost and manual operational overhead over the long retention window.</li>\n</ul>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Together, this combination gives the cheapest possible long-term storage, legally enforced immutability for compliance, and hands-off automation for the transition and eventual disposal of the records.</p>\n<div style=\"margin-top: 1rem;
