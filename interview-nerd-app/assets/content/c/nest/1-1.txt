{"title":"Project Structure Conventions","tags":["project-structure","feature-modules","core-module","shared-module"],"html":"<div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.5rem\">\nNest does not force a folder layout on you. It only requires that every module is reachable from <code>AppModule</code>. Everything else is convention &mdash; but the conventions matter, because a structure that made sense with five files becomes painful with five hundred, and by then it is expensive to change.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThe single most important decision is <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">group by feature, not by type</span>. Everything about orders lives together. Everything about users lives together.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nWhy Not Group by Type\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nMany people arriving from other frameworks start like this:\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">src/\n  controllers/\n    orders.controller.ts\n    users.controller.ts\n    payments.controller.ts\n  services/\n    orders.service.ts\n    users.service.ts\n    payments.service.ts\n  dto/\n    create-order.dto.ts\n    create-user.dto.ts</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nIt looks tidy and it works badly, for three reasons.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\n<span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Every change touches every folder.</span> Adding one field to an order means editing files in <code>controllers/</code>, <code>services/</code>, <code>dto/</code> and <code>entities/</code>. Your pull requests are scattered and hard to review.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\n<span style=\"font-weight: 600\">You cannot see boundaries.</span> Looking at <code>services/</code> tells you nothing about which service belongs to which part of the product, or which ones depend on each other.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\n<span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Deleting a feature is painful.</span> With feature folders you delete one directory. With type folders you hunt through six.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nFeature grouping fixes all three, and it lines up with how Nest modules already work &mdash; a module <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">is</span> a feature boundary, so the folder should match it.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nA Layout That Scales\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">src/\n  main.ts\n  app.module.ts\n  config/                      how settings are loaded and validated\n    configuration.ts\n    validation.schema.ts\n  core/                        infrastructure, imported ONCE at the root\n    core.module.ts\n    database/\n    logger/\n    health/\n  common/                      generic building blocks with no business logic\n    decorators/\n    filters/\n    guards/\n    interceptors/\n    pipes/\n    utils/\n  orders/                      a feature\n    orders.module.ts\n    orders.controller.ts\n    orders.service.ts\n    dto/\n      create-order.dto.ts\n      update-order.dto.ts\n      order-response.dto.ts\n    entities/\n      order.entity.ts\n    orders.controller.spec.ts\n    orders.service.spec.ts\n  users/                       another feature\n    ...\ntest/                          end-to-end tests</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nFour kinds of folder, each with a clear job.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nFeature Modules\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nA feature module owns one business capability. It contains its controller, its service, its DTOs and its entities, and it exports as little as possible.\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>typescript</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\"><i class=m>@Module</i>({\n  <i class=a>imports</i>: [<i class=f>TypeOrmModule</i>.<i class=f>forFeature</i>([<i class=f>OrderEntity</i>]), <i class=f>PaymentsModule</i>],\n  <i class=a>controllers</i>: [<i class=f>OrdersController</i>],\n  <i class=a>providers</i>: [\n    <i class=f>OrdersService</i>,\n    <i class=f>OrderPricingCalculator</i>,     <i class=c>// internal - NOT exported</i>\n    <i class=f>TypeOrmOrderRepository</i>,     <i class=c>// internal - NOT exported</i>\n  ],\n  <i class=a>exports</i>: [<i class=f>OrdersService</i>],     <i class=c>// the only public surface</i>\n})\n<i class=k>export</i> <i class=k>class</i> <i class=f>OrdersModule</i> {}</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nKeeping the export list short is the whole point. Because the calculator and repository are not exported, no other module can possibly depend on them, so you can rename or replace them freely.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\n<span style=\"font-weight: 600\">When to split a feature module:</span> it has more than roughly ten providers; its name contains \"and\"; two unrelated teams keep editing it; or its export list keeps growing, which means it has no clear job.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\n<span style=\"font-weight: 600\">When not to split:</span> a module with one provider used by one other module. That is ceremony, not structure. Merge it back.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThe Core Module\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nCore holds infrastructure that the whole application needs exactly once: the database connection, the logger, health checks, global filters and interceptors. It is imported <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">only by <code>AppModule</code></span>, never by a feature.\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>typescript</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\"><i class=m>@Module</i>({\n  <i class=a>imports</i>: [<i class=f>ConfigModule</i>, <i class=f>DatabaseModule</i>, <i class=f>LoggerModule</i>, <i class=f>HealthModule</i>],\n  <i class=a>providers</i>: [\n    { <i class=a>provide</i>: <i class=v>APP_FILTER</i>, <i class=a>useClass</i>: <i class=f>AllExceptionsFilter</i> },\n    { <i class=a>provide</i>: <i class=v>APP_INTERCEPTOR</i>, <i class=a>useClass</i>: <i class=f>LoggingInterceptor</i> },\n    { <i class=a>provide</i>: <i class=v>APP_GUARD</i>, <i class=a>useClass</i>: <i class=f>JwtAuthGuard</i> },\n  ],\n  <i class=a>exports</i>: [<i class=f>ConfigModule</i>, <i class=f>DatabaseModule</i>, <i class=f>LoggerModule</i>],\n})\n<i class=k>export</i> <i class=k>class</i> <i class=f>CoreModule</i> {}</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nNotice it re-exports the modules it imported. That means one <code>CoreModule</code> import in <code>AppModule</code> makes config, database and logging available, instead of repeating three imports everywhere.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nRegistering the global guard, filter and interceptor here rather than in <code>main.ts</code> matters. Registered as providers with the <code>APP_*</code> tokens, they go through the container and <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">can inject dependencies</span>. Registered with <code>app.useGlobalGuards(new AuthGuard())</code> in <code>main.ts</code>, you built them yourself, so they cannot inject anything &mdash; and most real guards need <code>Reflector</code> to read route metadata.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThe Common Folder\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nCommon holds generic, reusable pieces with <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">no business knowledge</span> &mdash; a logging interceptor, a <code>@CurrentUser()</code> decorator, a pagination helper, a serialisation interceptor.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nOften these are not a module at all, just files you import directly, because a decorator or a utility function does not need to be registered with the container.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThe rule that keeps this folder healthy: <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">if it mentions a business concept, it does not belong here</span>. An <code>OrderValidator</code> in <code>common/</code> is a sign the folder has become a dumping ground. Once that starts, <code>common/</code> ends up depending on features, features depend on <code>common/</code>, and you have circular dependencies everywhere.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nSome teams use a single <code>SharedModule</code> instead of a folder of loose files. Both work. The difference is that a module gives you one import and dependency injection; a folder is simpler when the contents are mostly decorators and pure functions.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nCore vs Common vs Shared\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThese three names get used inconsistently, so it helps to define them by their rules rather than their names:\n</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: disc\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Core</span> &mdash; things that must exist exactly once. Database connection, logger, global enhancers. Imported once, by <code>AppModule</code> only.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Common / Shared</span> &mdash; things safely reused many times. Decorators, pipes, utilities, base classes. Imported by many modules.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Feature</span> &mdash; a business capability. Imported only by <code>AppModule</code> or by another feature that genuinely needs it.</li>\n</ul>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThe test that decides: <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">\"what breaks if this is imported twice?\"</span> If a second import would give you two database pools or two schedulers, it is core. If a second import is harmless, it is shared.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nNaming Conventions\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nNest uses kebab-case filenames with a type suffix, and it is worth following even though nothing enforces it:\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">orders.module.ts          OrdersModule\norders.controller.ts      OrdersController\norders.service.ts         OrdersService\ncreate-order.dto.ts       CreateOrderDto\norder.entity.ts           OrderEntity\njwt-auth.guard.ts         JwtAuthGuard\nlogging.interceptor.ts    LoggingInterceptor\nparse-object-id.pipe.ts   ParseObjectIdPipe\nhttp-exception.filter.ts  HttpExceptionFilter\ncurrent-user.decorator.ts CurrentUser</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThe benefit is that the suffix tells you what a file is before you open it, and it makes search useful &mdash; searching <code>.guard.ts</code> finds every guard in the codebase. The CLI generates these names automatically, which is a good reason to use it.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nBarrel Files: Use With Care\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nA barrel file is an <code>index.ts</code> that re-exports everything in a folder, so you can write one tidy import instead of five.\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>typescript</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\"><i class=c>// orders/dto/index.ts</i>\n<i class=k>export</i> * <i class=k>from</i> <i class=s>&#x27;./create-order.dto&#x27;</i>;\n<i class=k>export</i> * <i class=k>from</i> <i class=s>&#x27;./update-order.dto&#x27;</i>;\n<i class=c>// then</i>\n<i class=k>import</i> { <i class=f>CreateOrderDto</i>, <i class=f>UpdateOrderDto</i> } <i class=k>from</i> <i class=s>&#x27;./dto&#x27;</i>;</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThey look harmless and they are the <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">number one cause of circular dependency errors</span> in Nest projects.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nHere is why. Importing one thing from a barrel loads <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">every</span> file the barrel re-exports. So if <code>orders/index.ts</code> exports both the service and the DTO, and <code>payments</code> imports just the DTO, it has actually loaded the service too &mdash; and everything the service imports. Two files that never mention each other end up in the same load cycle.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThe result is Nest failing at startup with \"cannot resolve dependency at index [0]\" and a blank name, which means the recorded type was <code>undefined</code> because the class had not finished loading.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nPractical guidance: barrels are fine for leaf folders that only contain types and DTOs. Avoid them for folders containing modules and services. And never create a barrel at the root of a feature that re-exports the module itself.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nPath Aliases\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nLong relative paths are unpleasant and break when you move a file. Path aliases fix that:\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>json</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\"><i class=c>// tsconfig.json</i>\n<i class=x>{</i>\n  <i class=a>&quot;compilerOptions&quot;</i><i class=x>:</i> <i class=x>{</i>\n    <i class=a>&quot;baseUrl&quot;</i><i class=x>:</i> <i class=s>&quot;./&quot;</i><i class=x>,</i>\n    <i class=a>&quot;paths&quot;</i><i class=x>:</i> <i class=x>{</i>\n      <i class=a>&quot;@app/common/*&quot;</i><i class=x>:</i> <i class=x>[</i><i class=s>&quot;src/common/*&quot;</i><i class=x>]</i><i class=x>,</i>\n      <i class=a>&quot;@app/core/*&quot;</i><i class=x>:</i>   <i class=x>[</i><i class=s>&quot;src/core/*&quot;</i><i class=x>]</i><i class=x>,</i>\n      <i class=a>&quot;@app/config&quot;</i><i class=x>:</i>   <i class=x>[</i><i class=s>&quot;src/config&quot;</i><i class=x>]</i>\n    <i class=x>}</i>\n  <i class=x>}</i>\n<i class=x>}</i></code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nOne warning: aliases are a <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">compile-time</span> feature. The emitted JavaScript still contains the literal string <code>'@app/common/utils'</code>, and Node does not understand it. The Nest CLI handles this for you, but if someone customises the build &mdash; running <code>tsc</code> directly, or writing a bespoke Dockerfile &mdash; you get \"cannot find module\" at runtime after a clean compile.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nWhere to Put Tests\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nNest's convention is unit tests <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">next to</span> the file they test, and end-to-end tests in a separate top-level <code>test/</code> folder.\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">src/orders/orders.service.ts\nsrc/orders/orders.service.spec.ts     &lt;- unit test, right beside it\ntest/orders.e2e-spec.ts               &lt;- end-to-end test</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nKeeping unit tests beside the source makes them much more likely to be updated, because you cannot miss them when editing the file. End-to-end tests live separately because they boot the whole application and use a different Jest configuration.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nWhen to Add More Layers\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nSome teams split each feature further, into domain, application, infrastructure and presentation folders:\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">orders/\n  domain/            pure business rules, no Nest imports at all\n    order.entity.ts\n    ports/order.repository.port.ts\n  application/       use cases, imports domain only\n    confirm-order.usecase.ts\n  infrastructure/    adapters, imports domain + libraries\n    typeorm-order.repository.ts\n  presentation/      the web surface\n    orders.controller.ts\n  orders.module.ts   the only place that wires ports to adapters</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThis is genuinely valuable when the domain has real rules and workflows, when the database or a third-party choice might change, or when several teams need enforced boundaries.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nIt is over-engineering for a CRUD service with three endpoints. You end up with four files and two mapping functions to express what one service and one entity would say more clearly, and your \"ports\" are abstractions with exactly one implementation that will never have a second.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nBeing able to say when <span style=\"font-weight: 600\">not</span> to apply this is the more senior answer. Over-applied architecture costs as much as none.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nKeeping AppModule Small\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>typescript</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\"><i class=m>@Module</i>({\n  <i class=a>imports</i>: [\n    <i class=f>CoreModule</i>,      <i class=c>// config, database, logger, global enhancers</i>\n    <i class=f>UsersModule</i>,\n    <i class=f>OrdersModule</i>,\n    <i class=f>PaymentsModule</i>,\n  ],\n})\n<i class=k>export</i> <i class=k>class</i> <i class=f>AppModule</i> {}</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThat is all it should be: a list. No controllers, no providers, no configuration. If <code>AppModule</code> is growing, whatever is being added belongs in <code>CoreModule</code> or in a feature.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nA short <code>AppModule</code> is also good documentation &mdash; someone new can read it in ten seconds and see what the application is made of.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nCommon Mistakes\n</h4>\n<ol style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: number\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Grouping by type.</span> Every change touches four folders and boundaries are invisible.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Business logic in <code>common/</code>.</span> Turns the shared folder into a dumping ground and creates cycles.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Barrel files everywhere.</span> The main cause of circular dependency errors.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Importing <code>CoreModule</code> from a feature.</span> It is meant to be imported once; a second import can create duplicate infrastructure.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Marking everything <code>@Global()</code>.</span> Convenient now, untraceable later, and it breaks small test modules.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">A fat <code>AppModule</code></span> with controllers and providers of its own.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Sharing entities as DTOs.</span> Leaks database columns and couples your API to your schema.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.4rem\"><span style=\"font-weight: 600\">Full hexagonal layering on a CRUD app.</span> Indirection with no payoff.</li>\n</ol>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem;
