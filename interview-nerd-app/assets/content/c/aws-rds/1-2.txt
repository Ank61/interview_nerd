Instead of choosing a db.r7g.2xlarge and paying for it around the clock, you declare a minimum and a maximum in <strong>Aurora Capacity Units</strong>, and the instance adjusts its own CPU, memory, and network allocation <strong>in place, in fine-grained steps, within seconds, without dropping connections</strong>. The ratio is deliberately similar to Aurora's memory-optimized instance families, so roughly speaking 8 ACUs ≈ 16 GiB ≈ a db.r-class 2xlarge-ish footprint. You configure:</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>Minimum capacity</strong> — the floor the instance never drops below. You pay for the capacity actually allocated at each instant, not the maximum you configured — the maximum is a guardrail, not a reservation. Scale-up is intentionally quick and can roughly double capacity in a few seconds when demand spikes. <strong>Scale-down is deliberately slow</strong> — capacity decreases gradually, typically taking on the order of 15 minutes to halve — because shrinking memory means shrinking the buffer cache, and rapidly discarding cached pages would cause a latency cliff and a burst of storage I/O. Understand this asymmetry: a workload with short, sharp spikes every few minutes will effectively sit near its peak ACU most of the time, because it never gets to scale down between spikes. That is a real and commonly missed cost trap.</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Two nuances worth stating. First, <strong>memory pressure is often the binding constraint, not CPU.</strong> If your working set needs 24 GiB, the instance will settle near 12 ACUs regardless of CPU idle time, because dropping lower would thrash. Second, scaling is per-instance: the writer and each reader scale independently based on their own load.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">The Minimum ACU Floor Is the Decision That Matters</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Everyone focuses on the maximum. The <strong>minimum</strong> is where the design judgment lives, because the floor determines the <strong>size of your buffer cache during quiet periods</strong> and therefore the shape of your recovery from idle.</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>Too low a floor starves the cache.</strong> At 0.5 ACUs you have roughly 1 GiB of memory. A query that would be a cache hit at 8 ACUs becomes a storage read, so latency is worse <em>and</em> your Aurora Standard I/O charges go up. On a cluster with a 40 GB working set, a 0.5 ACU floor means every quiet period cold-starts your cache.</li>\n<li><strong>Warm-up after idle is not instantaneous.</strong> Capacity can be added in seconds, but repopulating a buffer cache from storage takes as long as it takes to read the working set. The first burst of traffic after a quiet night hits a cold cache, so p99 spikes even though ACUs climbed promptly. If your workload has a sharp morning ramp, a higher floor is buying you warm cache, not idle CPU.</li>\n<li><strong>A 0 ACU floor allows auto-pause</strong> after a configurable period of inactivity, which is excellent for dev/test and truly idle tenants, but resuming from paused introduces a startup delay on the first connection. Never use it for anything latency-sensitive.</li>\n<li><strong>Too high a floor destroys the economics.</strong> A 16 ACU floor running 24×7 is, in ACU-hours, comparable to or more expensive than an equivalent provisioned instance — and a provisioned instance can be covered by a <strong>Reserved Instance</strong>, whereas ACU-hours cannot. If you find yourself setting a high floor because the workload is steady, that is the signal to use provisioned.</li>\n</ul>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">A practical starting heuristic: set the minimum at roughly <strong>one quarter to one eighth of your typical peak</strong>, and no lower than what holds your hot working set, then tune using the <code>ServerlessDatabaseCapacity</code> and <code>ACUUtilization</code> CloudWatch metrics over a couple of weeks.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem;
