Every \"memory\" an agent appears to have is you putting something back into the prompt. So the question is never \"does it remember\" — it is <em>what do you choose to put back in, and where did you keep it.</em>\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThe Two Kinds, And Why They Are Different Systems\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">SHORT-TERM (working memory)\n  WHAT   this conversation, this run&#x27;s tool results\n  WHERE  the context window\n  LASTS  one session\n  LIMIT  tokens\n  PROBLEM  it overflows\n  SOLVED BY  compression - windows, summaries, structured state\n\nLONG-TERM (persistent memory)\n  WHAT   facts that outlive the session - preferences, past\n         decisions, entity facts, what was tried before\n  WHERE  a database you own\n  LASTS  until deleted\n  LIMIT  relevance, not size\n  PROBLEM  retrieving the right thing, and staleness\n  SOLVED BY  retrieval - which means this is RAG over your\n             own history, with all the same problems\n\nTHESE ARE NOT THE SAME SYSTEM. Short-term is a COMPRESSION\nproblem. Long-term is a RETRIEVAL problem. Teams that treat\nthem as one thing end up with a vector store full of\nconversational noise that makes answers worse.</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nLong-term memory is usually worth splitting further: <strong>semantic</strong> (durable facts — \"prefers metric units\", \"account tier is enterprise\"), <strong>episodic</strong> (what happened in past sessions — \"we tried the Postgres migration in March and rolled it back\"), and occasionally <strong>procedural</strong> (learned how-tos). They are written and retrieved differently, and naming them shows you have thought past a single memory table.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nWhy Stuffing History Into The Prompt Fails\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">It works for ten turns. Then, in order:\n\n  COST     every turn re-sends the whole history. Turn 40\n           costs many times turn 4 for the same question, and\n           the total across a conversation grows roughly with\n           the SQUARE of its length.\n  LATENCY  more input tokens, slower prefill, every turn.\n  QUALITY  this is the one people miss. Long contexts suffer\n           from lost-in-the-middle, and irrelevant old turns\n           ACTIVELY DEGRADE the answer. More history is not\n           more helpful - past a point it is noise.\n  LIMIT    eventually it simply does not fit.\n\nAND IT STILL IS NOT LONG-TERM MEMORY. A new session starts\nempty no matter how much history you kept in the last one.</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nShort-Term: Four Techniques, Usually Combined\n</h4>\n<ol style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: decimal\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Sliding window.</strong> Keep the last N turns verbatim. Trivial, and it silently drops the constraint the user gave on turn 2.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Rolling summary plus window.</strong> Older turns collapse into a running summary; recent turns stay verbatim. The standard baseline, and good enough for most chat.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Structured state extraction.</strong> The strongest technique and the most underused. Instead of summarising in prose, maintain a small JSON object of what actually matters — the goal, confirmed constraints, decisions made, open questions. It is compact, stable, and does not drift the way repeated re-summarisation does.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Tool result compression.</strong> In agents this dominates everything else — observations are far bigger than dialogue. Cap and summarise every result before it enters the context.</li>\n</ol>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nA caution worth raising unprompted: <strong>repeated re-summarisation drifts.</strong> Summarising a summary five times loses specifics and can invent connective tissue. Structured state avoids this because you update fields rather than re-writing prose, and keeping the original first message verbatim anchors the whole thing.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nLong-Term: A Write Path And A Read Path\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">WRITE PATH - the hard half, and the half people skip.\n\n  WHAT TO SAVE is a judgement call, and the default should be\n  ALMOST NOTHING.
