What you want to be able to read off is the <strong>trajectory</strong>: what it decided at each step and why, which tools it called with what arguments, and where it went wrong. Agents fail in the middle — step four called the wrong tool because step three's result was misread — and without step-level spans you see only a bad final answer with no visible cause.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nSampling, And The One Rule That Matters\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nAt volume you cannot keep full traces for everything — they are large, because they contain prompts. So sample: <strong>100% of errors, 100% of guardrail trips and low-confidence answers, 100% of anything a user gave feedback on, and a small random baseline</strong> of everything else.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nThat last one has a sequencing problem: <strong>feedback arrives after the trace decision</strong>.
