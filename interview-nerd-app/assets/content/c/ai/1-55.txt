They failed at requirements.</strong> The model was fine. The team built something nobody needed, or something the security review would never have allowed, or something over documents that were never good enough to answer from.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThe One Question That Beats All The Others\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">  &quot;HOW IS THIS DONE TODAY, AND WHAT DOES GOOD LOOK LIKE?&quot;\n\nAsk that first and you get four things at once:\n\n  the CURRENT PROCESS, so you know what you are replacing\n  the PEOPLE who do it, who are your users and your experts\n  the BASELINE - how accurate and how fast it is now, which\n    is what you are actually competing with\n  the DEFINITION OF GOOD, in their words\n\nIf nobody can answer it, that is the finding. It usually\nmeans the problem has not been thought about yet, and no\namount of good engineering rescues that.</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nQuestions For The Business\n</h4>\n<ol style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: decimal\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>What decision or task does this change?</strong> If the answer is \"people would find it interesting\", stop. Something has to happen differently afterwards.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Who uses it, how often, and what do they do now?</strong> Five people occasionally is a different product from five thousand people daily.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>What does success look like, as a number?</strong> Agreed <em>before</em> building, or the bar becomes whatever you happen to achieve.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>What happens when it is wrong?</strong> <em>The most important question on this list</em> — see below.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>What is one use worth?</strong> This sets your cost budget. A few pence per answer is fine if it saves ten minutes; it is not fine at a million requests a day for something optional.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Who owns the content, and is it any good?</strong> The question that quietly decides whether the project works at all.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>What is the deadline, and why that date?</strong> \"A conference in six weeks\" tells you a lot about what you can promise.</li>\n</ol>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\n\"What Happens When It Is Wrong?\" Decides The Architecture\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">The same question, three different answers, three different\nsystems.\n\n  &quot;Someone notices and asks again.&quot;\n    -&gt; a simple pipeline. Ask this first — the answer can rule out an entire design.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>What is the classification of this content?</strong> Public, internal, confidential, regulated. Everything else follows from it.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Where do permissions live today</strong>, and can we read them programmatically? If permissions live in someone's head, you have a problem before you start.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Which identity system do we integrate with</strong>, and does it support acting on behalf of a user?</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>What must be logged, and what must not be?</strong> And for how long.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Do we need to explain decisions later?</strong> If yes, audit design starts now, not after launch.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>What is the incident process</strong> if it says something wrong, and who is on call for it?</li>\n</ul>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nGo And Look At The Documents\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nBefore agreeing to anything, <strong>open twenty of the actual files</strong>.
