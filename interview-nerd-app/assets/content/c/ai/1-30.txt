For open questions they do two bad things: they <em>punish</em> a correct answer written in different words, and they <em>reward</em> padding an answer with words from the reference. Saying that clearly is a good signal in an interview.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThree Levels Of Grading, Cheapest First\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">1. If the answer drifted onto a different topic, the questions you generate from it will not look like the question that was asked. It needs no reference answer, which makes it usable in production.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nWhen There Is Genuinely More Than One Right Answer\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nParaphrasing is the easy version of the problem. The harder version is a question where several <em>different</em> answers are all fine — a recommendation, a summary, a plan, a piece of advice. There is no fact list to check.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nFor these, write a <strong>rubric for that specific question</strong> instead of a reference answer: <em>must mention the notice period, must not promise a refund, must tell them who to contact</em>. You are describing the shape of an acceptable answer rather than the answer itself, and the grading becomes a set of yes-or-no checks again.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nTwo Habits That Make Scores Much More Reliable\n</h4>\n<ul style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: disc\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Pass or fail per criterion, then add up. Never a score out of ten.</strong> Models are poor at fine-grained numbers — ask for a 1-to-10 rating and almost everything comes back as 7 or 8, and the same answer scored twice gets different numbers. Five yes-or-no checks give you a score out of five that actually means something.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Compare two answers instead of scoring one.</strong> \"Which of these is better?\" is far more reliable than \"rate this\". Ideal when you are comparing two prompt versions. Just remember to run it in both orders, because judges favour whichever they see first.</li>\n</ul>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nOne more: <strong>tell the grader to ignore length, tone and formatting.</strong> Otherwise you are quietly grading style, and you will find your system slowly getting more verbose because that is what scored well.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nHow This Comes Up In Interviews\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">THE QUESTION\n  &quot;How do you score whether an answer is correct when there\n   are many valid phrasings?&quot;\n\nWHAT THE INTERVIEWER IS TESTING\n  Whether you have actually built a grader.
