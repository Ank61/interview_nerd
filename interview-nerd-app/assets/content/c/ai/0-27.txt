Metadata captures everything else: where it came from, who may read it, when it was written, what kind of thing it is, and which version of your pipeline produced it.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.5rem\">\nAlmost every hard requirement in an enterprise RAG system is satisfied by metadata rather than by embeddings. Permissions, citations, freshness, deletion, multi-tenancy, migration — none of these are semantic problems. If you get metadata wrong, you cannot bolt it on later without re-indexing, which is why this deserves real design attention before you build.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThe Six Categories Of Metadata\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">1. This is one of the most common production failures in RAG, and embeddings cannot help you because both documents are semantically about refunds.\n</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: disc\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>supersededBy</strong> — an explicit pointer from an old version to its replacement. But metadata can also <em>influence</em> ranking, which is often what you actually want:\n</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: disc\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Recency boost</strong> — newer content ranks higher without excluding older content entirely.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Authority boost</strong> — an official policy outranks a draft or a personal note on the same topic.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Proximity boost</strong> — a document from the user's own department or region ranks higher.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Type preference</strong> — a how-to guide over a meeting note, when the query looks procedural.</li>\n</ul>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nApply these <em>after</em> retrieval and reranking rather than trying to fold them into the vector search, so the boosts are explicit, tunable and testable rather than buried. And be careful: aggressive boosting can override genuine relevance, so measure it against your golden set like any other change.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nSchema Design And Evolution\n</h4>\n<ul style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: disc\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Capture generously at ingest.</strong> Storing a field you do not use costs almost nothing. Adding one later means re-ingesting from source — and the source may have changed, rotated credentials, or been decommissioned.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Denormalise the chunk text into metadata.</strong> You need it to build the prompt and to show the user, so storing it alongside the vector saves a second lookup on every query.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Watch cardinality.</strong> Low-cardinality fields (docType, department) filter efficiently. Very high-cardinality fields (a unique tag per chunk) are effectively useless as filters and bloat the index.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Version your schema.</strong> A metadataVersion field lets you detect chunks written by an older pipeline and backfill them incrementally rather than rebuilding everything.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Some changes are backfillable, some are not.</strong> Adding a derived field like docType can be backfilled from what you stored. Adding permissions cannot, because that data lived in the source system at ingest time. Know which is which before you launch.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Mind the size limits.</strong> Vector stores cap metadata size per record. Storing a full 2,000-token chunk plus a generated summary plus twenty tags can hit that ceiling.</li>\n</ul>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nHow This Comes Up In Interviews\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">QUESTIONS YOU WILL ACTUALLY BE ASKED\n  &quot;What metadata do you attach to each chunk, and why?&quot;\n  &quot;How do you handle per-user permissions?&quot;\n  &quot;Users are getting last year&#x27;s policy.
