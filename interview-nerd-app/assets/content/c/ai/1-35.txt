Online tells you whether it is actually better for people.</strong> They answer different questions and you need both.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nWhat Offline Cannot Tell You\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">1. WHAT REAL PEOPLE ACTUALLY ASK.\n   Your golden set is made of questions you imagined. Real\n   questions are shorter, vaguer, worse spelled, and often\n   about things you never covered. You wrote &quot;What is the\n   annual leave entitlement for full-time staff?&quot; and they\n   type &quot;holidays?&quot;.\n\n2. THE REAL MIX.\n   Your set is balanced across categories on purpose. Real\n   traffic is 70% one boring category. A change that helps\n   the hard questions and slightly hurts the common ones\n   looks GOOD offline and BAD in production, because you\n   weighted them equally and reality does not.\n\n3. WHETHER ANYONE FINDS IT USEFUL.\n   An answer can be more accurate and less useful. Offline\n   scoring cannot see whether people acted on it.\n\n4. SPEED AND COST UNDER REAL LOAD.\n   Your eval runs a few cases at a time. Production runs\n   thousands at once with real input sizes.\n\n5. HOW IT FITS THE WHOLE PRODUCT.\n   Longer answers may be better in isolation and worse in a\n   chat window on a phone.\n\n6. THE TAIL.\n   The 1% of strange inputs that produce most of your\n   incidents are, by definition, not in a set you wrote.\n\nBUT YOU STILL NEED OFFLINE. It is fast, cheap and safe, and\nyou cannot A/B test every prompt tweak - too slow, and you\nwould be experimenting on users with changes you have not\nchecked. OFFLINE IS THE GATE. Real questions. Real distribution. You find out\n  what your golden set never imagined, and nobody is exposed\n  to anything.\n\n  You get: how often they differ, where they differ, real\n  latency and cost for the new version, and a stack of\n  disagreements a human can review.\n\nTHIS IS THE MOST UNDERUSED TECHNIQUE IN THE WHOLE TOPIC.\nMost teams jump straight from an offline score to a 5%\nrollout and skip the free step in between.\n\nTHEN, AND ONLY THEN:\n  5%  -&gt; 25% -&gt; 50% -&gt; 100%, behind a feature flag, with a\n  kill switch and someone watching.</code></pre></div>\n</div>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nDecide The Metrics Before You Start\n</h4>\n<ul style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: disc\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>One primary metric</strong>, chosen in advance. The experience is inconsistent, and you cannot measure anything that spans a session — like whether they had to ask three times. <strong>Assign a user to a version and keep them there</strong> for the whole test.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nWhy AI A/B Tests Are Harder Than Normal Ones\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">NOISIER. Answers vary run to run, so you need more traffic\nand more time than a button-colour test to see a real\ndifference.\n\nTHE METRIC IS INDIRECT. You cannot observe &quot;was the answer\ngood&quot;.
