It is, loosely, a blend of everything in that text. So if a 2,000-token chunk covers five topics, its vector sits somewhere in the middle of all five — close to none of them. A query about topic three has to compete against the pull of the other four.\n</p>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">Imagine a chunk containing FIVE paragraphs:\n\n  1. Many open models cap at 512 tokens; OpenAI's embedding models go to 8,191. Exceed it and most implementations <strong>silently truncate</strong> — the tail is embedded as though it were not there, so that content can never be retrieved, and nothing errors. Always validate chunk length at index time and fail loudly.\n</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\">\n<span style=\"font-weight: 600; font-size: 1.1rem\">Your context budget divided by top-k :</span>\nChunk size and the number of chunks you retrieve multiply together. If you can afford 6,000 tokens of retrieved content, that is twelve 500-token chunks or six 1,000-token chunks or three 2,000-token chunks. You are choosing between <em>more sources</em> and <em>more context per source</em>, and that is a genuine product decision: comparative questions (\"how do these two policies differ?\") need many sources; explanatory questions need depth.\n</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\">\n<span style=\"font-weight: 600; font-size: 1.1rem\">Query length asymmetry :</span>\nQueries are short — often five to fifteen tokens. Chunks are hundreds. Embedding models trained for asymmetric retrieval handle this, but the mismatch grows with chunk size: a fifteen-token question and a two-thousand-token passage are very different objects. This is another reason very large chunks retrieve worse than intuition suggests.\n</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\">\n<span style=\"font-weight: 600; font-size: 1.1rem\">Reranking changes the calculus :</span>\nA cross-encoder reranker reads query and chunk together, so it is far less affected by dilution than embedding similarity is. If you rerank, you can retrieve larger chunks (or more of them) and let the reranker sort it out. Without reranking, chunk size matters much more.\n</li>\n</ol>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nOverlap — What It Is And What It Actually Fixes\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">Overlap means consecutive chunks SHARE some text at the seam.\n\n  Chunk 1: tokens    0 - 500\n  Chunk 2: tokens  450 - 950      &lt;- 50 tokens repeated\n  Chunk 3: tokens  900 - 1400\n\nWHAT IT SOLVES: an answer sitting exactly on a boundary.\n\n  WITHOUT overlap:\n    Chunk 1 ends: &quot;...employees may claim travel expenses&quot;\n    Chunk 2 starts: &quot;up to a limit of 500 per trip...&quot;\n    NEITHER chunk answers &quot;what is the travel expense limit?&quot;\n    The information exists and is unretrievable.\n\n  WITH overlap:\n    Chunk 2 starts: &quot;...employees may claim travel expenses up\n                     to a limit of 500 per trip...&quot;\n    Now one chunk contains the complete statement.\n\nWHAT IT COSTS:\n  Storage       ~10-20% more chunks and vectors.\n  Duplicate     Two overlapping chunks both match, so your top-5\n  retrieval     may contain the same text twice - wasting slots\n                and context budget. Three ways out:\n</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: disc\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Parent-child retrieval</strong> — embed small for precise search, return the large parent for context. The single best answer to \"how do you choose chunk size?\", because it dissolves the question.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Sentence-window retrieval</strong> — index individual sentences, expand to neighbours on retrieval. Maximum precision plus natural context.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Variable chunk size</strong> — let structure decide. A three-sentence definition and a two-page procedure are naturally different lengths, and forcing both to 800 tokens is worse than letting each be its own section.</li>\n</ul>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nPractical Traps\n</h4>\n<ul style=\"margin-top: 1.0rem; margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style-type: disc\">\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Sizing in characters instead of tokens.</strong> The limits that actually bind — embedding input length, context budget — are token limits. Character sizing works for English prose and breaks on code, JSON and non-English text, where the ratio is completely different, causing silent truncation exactly where you least expect it.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Forgetting that overlap causes duplicate retrieval.</strong> Two overlapping chunks both match the query and both land in your top five, so you have wasted a slot and are paying for the same text twice. Deduplicate by content or by parent after retrieval.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Overlap larger than roughly a quarter of chunk size.</strong> At that point you are mostly storing duplicates, inflating your index and your bill for a shrinking benefit.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Ignoring the header you prepend.</strong> If you add a heading path to each chunk, that counts toward the token limit. A 500-token target plus a 60-token header is 560, and if your ceiling is 512 you are silently truncating every chunk.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Changing size without re-indexing.</strong> There is no in-place migration; a size change means re-chunking and re-embedding the whole corpus.</li>\n</ul>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nHow This Comes Up In Interviews\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">QUESTIONS YOU WILL ACTUALLY BE ASKED\n  &quot;What chunk size do you use?&quot;\n  &quot;What happens if chunks are too small or too large?&quot;\n  &quot;Why do you need overlap, and how much?&quot;\n  &quot;How would you pick the right size for a new corpus?&quot;\n\nWHAT THE INTERVIEWER IS TESTING\n  Whether you can explain the MECHANISM - specifically signal\n  dilution - or whether you just repeat &quot;1000 with 200 overlap&quot;\n  because that is the framework default.
