If you only measure the final answer, then when it is wrong you have no idea whether the right document was never found, or was found and buried at position 30, or was sitting right there in the prompt and the model ignored it. Those are three different bugs with three different fixes.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.5rem\">\nAnd it matters because <strong>most bad RAG answers are retrieval failures</strong>, not generation failures. People spend weeks on prompts for a problem that was upstream all along.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThe Setup: Label Which Document Should Come Back\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nFor each test question, write down which document or chunk actually contains the answer. Then you can ask a simple question that has nothing to do with the model: <em>did retrieval find it, and where in the list?</em>\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThe Four Metrics, And What Each One Is For\n</h4>\n<div style=\"padding: 2px; border-radius: 8px; font-size: 14px\">\n<div class=\"code\"><div class=\"code-head\"><span>code</span><button class=\"copy\" type=\"button\">Copy</button></div><pre><code class=\"hljs\">RECALL@k  - of the documents that SHOULD have been found,\n            how many appeared in the top k?\n\n  THE MOST IMPORTANT ONE FOR RAG.
