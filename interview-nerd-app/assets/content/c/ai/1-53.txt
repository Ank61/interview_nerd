Audit logs are <em>evidence</em>.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Different retention.</strong> Debug logs live for days or weeks. Audit records may need years.</li>\n<li style=\"margin-top: 0.5rem\"><strong>Different shape.</strong> Debug logs are verbose and unstructured. Say the first plainly and be honest about the second. In practice regulators care far more about <em>what information the decision was based on</em> and <em>whether a human was involved</em> than about the model's internals.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nRelated: never claim the system would produce the same answer again. It is non-deterministic. What you can say is <em>these were the inputs and this was the output</em>, which is a claim you can actually support.\n</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: -0.025em\">\nThe Retention Conflict, And How To Resolve It\n</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nAudit rules may require keeping records for years. Data protection rules push you to minimise and to honour deletion requests. These genuinely conflict, and the answer is not to pick one silently.\n</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75; margin-top: 1.0rem\">\nUsually a legal obligation to retain records overrides an erasure request for <em>those specific records</em> — but that has to be a decision your legal team made and documented, with the scope written down, rather than an assumption an engineer made.
