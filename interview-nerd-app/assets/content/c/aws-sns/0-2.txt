Choosing the protocol is the most consequential decision you make per subscriber, because it determines what happens when that subscriber is slow, broken or offline. This topic walks the list, and the theme running through all of it is: <strong>if losing a message would be bad, put a queue in the path</strong>.</p>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">Amazon SQS — The One That Matters Most</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Amazon SQS is a managed message queue: a durable line of messages that your own code pulls from at whatever pace it can manage. Subscribing a queue to a topic means SNS's job ends the instant the message is safely in the queue — a handoff that takes milliseconds and almost never fails.</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">That is what makes it the durable choice. If your worker is deploying, crashed, throttled or simply slow, the messages pile up in the queue (retention is up to 14 days) and are processed when it recovers. Nothing is lost, and the queue gives you back-pressure for free: a growing <code>ApproximateNumberOfMessagesVisible</code> metric is your early warning and your autoscaling signal.</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>Confirmation.</strong> Automatic — ownership is established through IAM, not a click.</li>\n<li><strong>Required permission.</strong> The <em>queue's</em> access policy must allow <code>sqs:SendMessage</code> for principal <code>sns.amazonaws.com</code>, ideally conditioned on <code>aws:SourceArn</code> equal to the topic ARN. Forget this and Subscribe still succeeds while every delivery silently fails.</li>\n<li><strong>Cross-account.</strong> Fully supported: the topic policy allows the other account to subscribe, and the queue policy allows the topic to send.</li>\n<li><strong>Gotcha.</strong> By default the queue receives the SNS JSON envelope, not your raw body, so your consumer must read <code>body.Message</code>. Turn on <strong>RawMessageDelivery</strong> if you want exactly what you published.</li>\n<li><strong>FIFO rule.</strong> A FIFO topic can only deliver to FIFO queues; a standard topic can only deliver to standard queues.</li>\n</ul>\n<h4 style=\"margin-top: 1.5rem; font-size: 1.25rem; font-weight: 600\">AWS Lambda — Convenient, With Bounded Retries</h4>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Subscribing a Lambda function means SNS invokes it <strong>asynchronously</strong> — SNS hands the event to Lambda's async invocation path and considers delivery complete. There is no event source mapping and no polling; your function's handler receives an event with a <code>Records</code> array where each record has an <code>Sns</code> object containing Message, MessageAttributes, Subject and Timestamp.</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">The subtlety is the retry story, which has two layers. SNS retries only if it could not <em>hand off</em> the invocation. Once Lambda has accepted it, failures inside your function are Lambda's problem, and Lambda's asynchronous invocation retries a failed execution <strong>twice</strong> (three attempts total) with delays, then discards the event unless you have configured a Lambda destination or dead-letter queue. It does not retry forever, and there is no queue holding your event in the meantime.</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>Confirmation.</strong> Automatic.</li>\n<li><strong>Required permission.</strong> A resource-based policy on the function allowing <code>lambda:InvokeFunction</code> from <code>sns.amazonaws.com</code> with a SourceArn condition — added by <code>aws lambda add-permission</code>.</li>\n<li><strong>When to prefer SQS in front.</strong> If throughput could exceed your concurrency limit, if you need more than three attempts, or if you want to batch. This is the right choice for integrating with something outside AWS, or with a partner's system, and the wrong choice for anything that must not be lost.</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>Confirmation required.</strong> Your endpoint receives a POST with <code>Type: SubscriptionConfirmation</code> and must fetch the <code>SubscribeURL</code> (or call ConfirmSubscription with the token) before any notification arrives.</li>\n<li><strong>Respond fast with 2xx.</strong> SNS treats a non-2xx status, a connection failure or a slow response as a failed delivery. It has a topic of its own later in this guide.</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\"><strong>Mobile push</strong> (protocol <code>application</code>) sends notifications to an installed app through the vendor's push service — <strong>APNs</strong> for Apple, <strong>FCM</strong> for Android, and others such as ADM for Amazon devices and Baidu.
