You choose it at graph time or alarm time; it is not baked into the data.</p>\n<ul style=\"margin-left: 1.5rem; list-style: disc; line-height: 2\">\n<li><strong>Sum.</strong> Add every value in the bucket. Period and statistic always work as a pair: \"the <em>Sum</em> over a <em>5-minute</em> period\" is a complete instruction; either alone is not.</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Period changes what you see. Sum over 60 seconds gives requests per minute; Sum over 3600 seconds gives requests per hour and the number is sixty times larger, which regularly confuses people comparing two graphs. Period also changes what you can detect: a one-minute burst to 100% CPU inside a 5-minute Average bucket may appear as a modest 30%. And period drives alarms directly — an alarm evaluates once per period, so a 5-minute period alarm needing 3 data points takes 15 minutes to fire. High-resolution metrics can be graphed at 1, 5, 10, 30 seconds, and alarms on them can evaluate every 10 or 30 seconds instead of every 60.</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">Use high resolution only where sub-minute reaction genuinely matters — a latency-critical trading path, a fast-scaling queue worker. Each source metric gets a short id such as <code>m1</code>, and the expression references those ids.</p>\n<p style=\"line-height: 1.75\">The classic use is an <strong>error rate</strong>.
